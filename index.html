In short

  • The game is made for everyone from the age of 3, children and families included. Once, it asks what year you were born.
  • The game keeps your progress and settings on your phone. There is no account of its own. The one server of ours is a counter of lanterns (below), which the game talks to only if a grown-up turns that on.
  • We do not ask for, and the game does not collect, a name, an email address, contacts, photos, a camera, a microphone, a location or anything you type. There is no chat and no way to talk to other players.
  • The game also checks your phone’s date, to open a festival at its time of year and to put a fresh order on the board each day you play. It uses the date on your phone only: the game does not send it to us, and the play statistics do not include it. (The save remembers the day the day’s order was last made; the copy in your Google account, if you turn that on, holds the save, so Google holds that day too.)
  • Play statistics are off. A grown-up can switch them on in Settings: they count game moments only (no names, nothing typed) and one tap switches them off again.
  • Lanterns that players everywhere light together are off. A grown-up can switch them on in Settings: then the game sends our counter how many orders and contracts you delivered (a number) and the game’s version, and reads back the total. Each request also carries your phone’s internet address, as every web request does; what the counter does with it, and what its host keeps, is explained below. We cannot tell who sent what, and one tap switches it off again.
  • A player who says they are 13 or over can choose to save their game to their own Google account with Google Play Games. It is optional and off until it is turned on, and it is never offered to, or started for, anybody else.
  • You can choose to watch a short video for a bonus. The videos come from Google AdMob and are set up for children: they are never personalised, and the game does not ask for the Android advertising ID. That is the same for every player.
  • “Sakura Diorama”, “Neon Garden”, “Pumpkin Patch” and “Cozy Supporter” are one-time purchases handled by Google Play. We never see your payment details.
  • A purchase, turning on play statistics, the lanterns everyone lights together or the copy in your Google account, deleting that copy, and this privacy policy’s web page are behind a question for grown-ups.

We cannot see these files. If Android’s backup is turned on on your phone, Android may copy them to your Google account’s backup so that your game comes back on a new phone. That is a feature of Android and Google, not of this game, and you can turn it off in your phone’s settings.

The game also checks your phone’s date, to open a festival at its time of year and to make the day’s order, one fresh order on the board for each day you play (the date decides which goods it asks for). It uses the date on your phone only: the game does not send it to us, and the play statistics do not include it. The save remembers the date the day’s order was last made or delivered, so that the next one is made on a later day; the copy in your Google account, if you turn that on, holds the save and so holds that date too, and so does Android’s own backup if that is on. The copy in your Google account and the play statistics each note when they were made, as described below.

Your birth year

The first time you play, the game asks “What year were you born?”. You can type a year or skip it. The game keeps only whether your answer means “13 and over” or not (not the year itself), on your phone, and sends it nowhere, not even in the play statistics. Skipping, or a year the game cannot believe, counts as a child. The answer decides whether the copy in your Google account (below) is offered and, for now, whether the play statistics are. It changes nothing else: the videos, the purchases and the game are the same for every answer. A grown-up can answer again in Settings > About, behind the question for grown-ups.

Play statistics (optional, off unless a grown-up turns them on)

In Settings a grown-up can switch on “Share play statistics”, after the question for grown-ups and a short card that says what is counted. For now the switch is offered only to players who said they are 13 or over. While it is off the game does not start the software that counts and counts nothing; one tap turns it off again and stops it at once.

While it is on, the game counts game moments and sends the counts to Google Analytics for Firebase (Google). A count is a kind of moment and a few numbers from the game’s own data, for example “an order was delivered”, “a milestone was reached”, “a festival opened”, “a lantern was lit”, how long a session lasted, which of the nine panels was opened, whether a video bonus was asked for and how it ended. It holds no name, nothing you typed, no age and no birth year, and it is never about a purchase or about the copy in your Google account.

Google’s software also collects some information by itself, automatically, and sends it to Google, encrypted:

  • your IP address, from which Google works out the country (and may estimate the general location of the phone);
  • when the game is opened and for how long, and when it is updated;
  • your phone’s model, its Android version, the version of the game and the language;
  • a random number that belongs to the game on your phone, not to you (the app instance ID);
  • which Google Play link or page brought the install, when Google Play says (the install referrer);
  • because the game’s software for purchases and videos is part of it, Google Analytics may also count a purchase (with the product and its price) or a video’s events by itself.

The game switches off the advertising ID, ads personalisation and every advertising consent, and Google Analytics is set up with Google signals and every data sharing setting off; the counts are not linked to Google Ads, AdMob or Google Play. Google keeps the counts for two months and is responsible for what it does with them: see Google’s Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. We receive the counts, not who sent them, and cannot tell which phone or person a count came from, so there is nothing for us to look up or delete for one player; turning the switch off stops anything more from being counted.

Lanterns everyone lights together (optional, off unless a grown-up turns it on)

In Settings a grown-up can switch on “Light lanterns with everyone”, after the question for grown-ups and a short card that says what is sent. It is offered to every player, whatever the birth-year question was answered. While it is off the game makes no request to our counter at all; one tap turns it off again and stops it at once.

While it is on, the game adds one to a number on your phone for every order or contract delivered while the game is open (by you or by a courier). When there are deliveries to report, it sends the number to our counter at most once every three minutes while you play (200 at most in one go), and tries once more when the game is paused or closed (the phone may stop the game before that finishes, and then the number waits on your phone and goes the next time the game is open). If the counter cannot be reached, the number waits on your phone, and the game tries again a minute later, and again, for as long as the switch is on and the game is open. The game also asks the counter for the total: when the switch is turned on, when the game opens, when you come back to it and the last total it read is five minutes old or more, and every ten minutes. Every player who has turned it on adds to the same total, and the total lights the same string of lanterns in every one of their games. A player who has not turned it on loses nothing: we intend a later update of the game to light the lanterns that players have lit for everyone.

What leaves your phone is the number, which festival it is for, the game’s version, and the internet (IP) address that every request to a web server carries. No account, name, number that belongs to you, the phone or the game, cookie or typed text goes with it, and never your birth-year answer. The counter is a small server of ours. It writes down one running total for each festival and nothing else (not the address, the version or the time, and no record of any player), so we cannot tell who sent a count. It uses the address for one thing besides answering: to slow down a flood of requests from one place. For that it holds each recent address, with the time of its latest requests, in the memory of the running server only, for as long as Cloudflare keeps that copy of the server running (we do not control that: it can be minutes or many hours), and it forgets an address that has been quiet for ten minutes once the list has grown long. It never writes the address to storage, and never uses it to find out who you are, to contact anyone or to build a profile.

The counter runs on Cloudflare, which hosts it. Cloudflare sees each connection, including the address, and may keep short technical records of it under its own Privacy Policy. We have switched off every log and per-request record that Cloudflare lets us switch off for the counter (Workers Logs, traces, Logpush and tail consumers). Cloudflare keeps its own totals for every server it hosts, such as how many requests and errors there were, and may keep short technical records of each connection under its own policy; the counter adds nothing of its own to what Cloudflare keeps.

Turning it off stops everything at once: nothing more is sent or asked for, the lanterns that are lit stay lit, and the count not yet sent is dropped. Turning it on again starts from zero. Android’s own backup, if it is on, copies the game’s settings and its save, so a game that is restored on another phone is joined there as it was here, with the count that had not been sent yet (500 at the most); turn the switch off in Settings on that phone to stop it.

Saving your game with Google Play Games (optional, for players 13 and over)

A player who said they are 13 or over sees, in Settings, a card “Save to Google Play Games”. It is off until they turn it on, after the question for grown-ups. Nobody else sees it, and the game does not start the Google Play Games software for anybody else.

While it is on, the game keeps one copy of the game in the player’s Google account (Google Play Games “saved games”), and reads it back when the game opens or after a while away, so the game can continue on another phone. The copy holds the game (the islands, machines, goods, coins, decor, helpers and counters, the day’s order and the day it was made for and, if you have turned on the lanterns everyone lights together, the count of deliveries not yet sent to our counter and the last total read back), a random game number, a count of how many times it was saved with a random number for each save, the time it was saved, a short description (“Comfort 112, 7 h 5 min played”) and the time played; it holds no purchases, no settings and no birth-year note, and no picture. Google signs the player in when the game opens and holds the copy for the player’s account: Google Play Games collects what it describes for its saved games (the player’s gamer profile, the saved game and diagnostics) and its terms and privacy policy apply to that. We do not receive the copy.

If the phone and the account each hold a different game, the game never decides: it shows both side by side and the player chooses (after the question for grown-ups); the one that was not chosen is kept on the phone as a spare copy. “Stop saving” in Settings stops it (the game does not start the software again until it is turned on again), and “Stop and delete the copy in my Google account” (after the question for grown-ups) also deletes the copy; Google Play Games’ own tools and the phone’s Google settings can delete a player’s saved games and sign out too.

Videos (ads)

You can choose to watch a short video, and only then, to start a Sunny spell or to double what the game gathered while you were away. The game never starts a video by itself, and a Cozy Supporter never needs to watch one.

The videos are shown by Google AdMob. Because the game is for children as well, it tells Google that every request is for children, whatever the birth-year question was answered: ads are limited to the “General audiences” rating, they are not personalised, and the game’s package does not ask for the Android advertising ID (its permission is removed). There is no consent form because nothing is personalised.

Google’s Mobile Ads software is a part of the game: Android starts a small piece of it whenever the game opens, as it does for every app that contains it. The game itself switches the software on, and asks Google for a video, only once the tutorial is done and ten minutes have been played with the game open; from then on that happens every time the game opens, except in a game that opens as a Cozy Supporter’s, where it does not happen at all. (If you become a Cozy Supporter while the game is open, the software that is already running stays on until the game is closed.) Before that, the game does not ask the software to start or to fetch anything.

To show a video and to keep it safe from fraud, Google’s Mobile Ads software in the game collects some information from your phone and sends it to Google, encrypted, automatically. The list follows Google’s own published description of that software:

  • your IP address, which Google may use to estimate the general location of the phone;
  • how you use the app and the video, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy use;
  • device identifiers such as the app set ID (a number that belongs to the game on your phone, not to you).

We do not receive this information and we add nothing of our own to it. Google is responsible for what it does with it: see Google’s Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

If you would rather not have videos at all, you never have to watch one, and a Cozy Supporter gets the bonuses without them.

Purchases

“Sakura Diorama”, “Neon Garden”, “Pumpkin Patch” and “Cozy Supporter” are bought through Google Play, by a grown-up. Google takes the payment and shows its own purchase screen; we never see your card or payment details. Google tells the game whether the purchase was paid, and the game keeps a short list of which products you own, on your phone. We do not keep purchase tokens, order numbers or your Google account details. “Restore purchases” asks Google Play which purchases belong to the Google account on your phone, and the game asks it the same, and for the prices, whenever it opens, so that a purchase made on another phone, or one that was still waiting for payment, is not lost. The coins in the game are only a game currency: they cannot be bought and they are not money.

Children

The game is made for everyone from the age of 3, children included. We designed it to follow the rules for apps for children, including the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Google Play’s Families policy: the game has no account of its own, no chat, no location and no personalised ads for anybody; it asks for no personal information; a child, and a player whose age the game does not know, never sees the copy in a Google account and the game never starts that software for them; play statistics are off unless a grown-up turns them on, and for now are not offered to a child; the lanterns everyone lights together are off unless a grown-up turns them on, and what the game then sends is a number and the game’s version, which say nothing about who sent them, besides the internet address that every request carries; and what leaves the game (a purchase, the copy in a Google account, the play statistics, the lanterns’ count and this page) is behind a question for grown-ups. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you are a parent or guardian and think a child has sent us personal information, write to us at the address above and we will answer and, where we can, act on it.

The videos, purchases, play statistics and Google Play Games involve Google, which has its own policies for children’s accounts; Google Play’s settings (and Family Link) let a parent decide what a child may buy or install. The lanterns’ counter runs on Cloudflare, which hosts it and holds nothing of a child’s beyond the connection itself.

Where information goes and how it is protected

Information collected by Google’s software (the videos, and the play statistics and the copy in a Google account when they are on) goes to Google, which may process it in any country where it operates. The lanterns’ count, when that is on, goes to our counter on Cloudflare’s network, which may handle it in any country where Cloudflare operates; we keep the totals and nothing about who sent them. Everything is sent encrypted (TLS). The game’s own files stay in the game’s private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read on a normal, unmodified phone. No way of storing or sending information is completely secure, so we cannot promise absolute security.

Keeping and deleting information

  • On your phone: until you use Start a new game (progress only), clear the app’s storage or uninstall the game.
  • In your Google account’s backup: until you delete the backup or turn it off in your phone’s settings.
  • The copy in your Google account (Google Play Games): until you stop saving and delete it in Settings, delete it with Google Play Games’ own tools, or delete your Google account’s games data.
  • Play statistics: Google holds the counts for two months. We keep no copy that identifies anyone.
  • Information collected by Google’s software for the videos: Google holds it under its own policies and retention periods. We keep no copy, so we cannot delete it for you.
  • The lanterns’ counter: one running total for each festival, which we mean to keep as long as that festival is in the game (nothing deletes it by itself), and no record of any player, so there is nothing to delete for one player; turning the switch off stops anything more from being added. The address of a request is held only in the memory of the running server, with the time of its latest requests, to slow down a flood from one place: it is never written to storage and is lost when Cloudflare stops that copy of the server (we do not control when: minutes or many hours), and an address that has been quiet for ten minutes is dropped once the list is long. Cloudflare, which hosts the counter, may keep its own short records of the connection under its own policy.
  • Emails to us: kept as long as needed to answer you and deal with the matter, then deleted.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the EEA, the UK, Switzerland, California and other US states), you or your child may have rights over personal information: to see it, correct it, delete it, limit or object to its use, take a copy and complain to your data protection authority. The game gives us no name or contact detail, and the count it can send us says nothing about who sent it, so there is nothing for us to look up or delete (the internet address of a request is used only as described under the lanterns’ counter). For the information Google’s software collects (the videos, the play statistics, the copy in your Google account), Google holds it and its tools (the links above and your Google account’s settings) are how to use these rights; for the records Cloudflare may keep of the connection to the lanterns’ counter, see Cloudflare’s Privacy Policy. If you are not sure where to start, write to us and we will help you find the right place.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy, for example when we add a feature. The effective date above changes when we do, and a change that collects more information is published before the update that needs it is released.
