In short

  • The game is made for children from the age of 3, and for families.
  • The game keeps your progress and settings on your phone. There are no accounts, no sign-in and no server of ours.
  • We do not ask for, and the game does not collect, a name, an email address, contacts, photos, a camera, a microphone, a location or anything you type. There is no chat and no way to talk to other players.

  • “Sakura Diorama” and “Cozy Supporter” are one-time purchases handled by Google Play. We never see your payment details.

Videos (ads)

You can choose to watch a short video, and only then, to start a Sunny spell or to double what the game gathered while you were away. The game never starts a video by itself, and a Cozy Supporter never needs to watch one.

The videos are shown by Google AdMob. Because the game is made for children, it tells Google that every request is for children: ads are limited to the “General audiences” rating, they are not personalised, and the game’s package does not ask for the Android advertising ID (its permission is removed). There is no consent form because nothing is personalised.

Google’s Mobile Ads software is a part of the game: Android starts a small piece of it whenever the game opens, as it does for every app that contains it. The game itself switches the software on, and asks Google for a video, only once the tutorial is done and ten minutes have been played with the game open; from then on that happens every time the game opens, except in a game that opens as a Cozy Supporter’s, where it does not happen at all. (If you become a Cozy Supporter while the game is open, the software that is already running stays on until the game is closed.) Before that, the game does not ask the software to start or to fetch anything.

To show a video and to keep it safe from fraud, Google’s Mobile Ads software in the game collects some information from your phone and sends it to Google, encrypted, automatically. The list follows Google’s own published description of that software:

  • your IP address, which Google may use to estimate the general location of the phone;
  • how you use the app and the video, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy use;
  • device identifiers such as the app set ID (a number that belongs to the game on your phone, not to you).

We do not receive this information and we add nothing of our own to it. Google is responsible for what it does with it: see Google’s Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

If you would rather not have videos at all, you never have to watch one, and a Cozy Supporter gets the bonuses without them.

Purchases

“Sakura Diorama” and “Cozy Supporter” are bought through Google Play, by a grown-up. Google takes the payment and shows its own purchase screen; we never see your card or payment details. Google tells the game whether the purchase was paid, and the game keeps a short list of which products you own, on your phone. We do not keep purchase tokens, order numbers or your Google account details. “Restore purchases” asks Google Play which purchases belong to the Google account on your phone, and the game asks it the same, and for the prices, whenever it opens, so that a purchase made on another phone, or one that was still waiting for payment, is not lost. The coins in the game are only a game currency: they cannot be bought and they are not money.

Children

The game is made for children from the age of 3. We designed it to follow the rules for apps for children, including the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Google Play’s Families policy: it has no accounts, no chat, no location, no analytics and no personalised ads, it asks for no personal information, and the two things that leave the game (a purchase and this page) are behind a question for grown-ups. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you are a parent or guardian and think a child has sent us personal information, write to us at the address above and we will answer and, where we can, act on it.

The videos and purchases involve Google, which has its own policies for children’s accounts; Google Play’s settings (and Family Link) let a parent decide what a child may buy or install.

Where information goes and how it is protected

Information collected by Google’s software (the videos) goes to Google, which may process it in any country where it operates. It is sent encrypted (TLS). The game’s own files stay in the game’s private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read on a normal, unmodified phone. No way of storing or sending information is completely secure, so we cannot promise absolute security.

Keeping and deleting information

  • On your phone: until you use Start a new game (progress only), clear the app’s storage or uninstall the game.
  • In your Google account’s backup: until you delete the backup or turn it off in your phone’s settings.
  • Information collected by Google’s software for the videos: Google holds it under its own policies and retention periods. We keep no copy, so we cannot delete it for you.
  • Emails to us: kept as long as needed to answer you and deal with the matter, then deleted.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the EEA, the UK, Switzerland, California and other US states), you or your child may have rights over personal information: to see it, correct it, delete it, limit or object to its use, take a copy and complain to your data protection authority. The game gives us no name or contact detail and sends nothing to us, so there is nothing for us to look up or delete. For the information Google’s software collects, Google holds it and its tools (the links above) are how to use these rights. If you are not sure where to start, write to us and we will help you find the right place.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy, for example when we add a feature. The effective date above changes when we do, and a change that collects more information is published before the update that needs it is released.
